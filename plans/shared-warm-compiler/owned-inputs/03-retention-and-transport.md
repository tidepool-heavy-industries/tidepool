## Wave A — physical ownership and granularity

Measure the existing producing row/arena/directory layout before choosing packing. The default target for new independently movable value interfaces is one value-sized immutable leaf owner for its own interface/sidecar/requirements, with shared dependency rows. A large compiled cell must not force a tiny escaped value to retain every old baseline or sibling output. Promotion of new output files from staging may copy new bytes once; account that cost explicitly.

Avoid one directory/arena per semantic graph reference. Choose physical ownership granularity against measured FD/inode/backing retention. Bounded chunks may be used if the supervisor records their workload/resource purpose, maximum stranded backing and growth evidence; do not introduce an arbitrary valid-program byte limit. A huge single value still legitimately retains its own huge bytes. Group/native graphs continue sharing their actual exact carriers and required edges; no repacking of an entire transitive graph on every cell/drop.

Keep exact selected row handles when complete owners fit, detach only necessary residual references, and preserve source ordering/selection precedence already established by the logical owner. Physical footprint deduplication must not collapse distinct acquisition issuers or change missing overrides. Preserve the rewrite handoff's optional-root versus required-child rules; unused custody may be stored without being materialized, validated as consumed namespace or advertised as an optional root.

After this parcel, independent review primary goal is final-borrower destruction. Secondary questions: whole-directory/arena pinning, recursive destructor depth and a hidden actor/runtime owner cycle. Repair and fold, then remeasure a growing history including teardown. A final retained row count linear in n does not establish linear cumulative work or bounded backing.
